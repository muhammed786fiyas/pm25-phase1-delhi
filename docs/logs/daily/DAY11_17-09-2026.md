## Key decisions

All four confirmed with Muhammed before any code was written:

- **Tuning split into its own script/stage**, not folded into the fit script -- so the validation stage depends on the tuning output directly and re-running the cheap fit doesn't drag the expensive search with it.
- **Production hyperparameters come from a fresh full-data Optuna search** over all 42 stations, not from reusing whichever block's set scored best. No held-out test set is left to protect once the deliverable model is being built.
- **Random CV uses the full-data hyperparameter set for all 42 folds.** The block rule ("station *i* gets its block's Hk") has nothing to key on for row-level folds containing rows from all 42 stations, and guarding the deliberately-leaky arm against hyperparameter leakage would be incoherent. Consequence flagged for any writeup: unlike the LME comparison, the two arms do **not** share hyperparameters, so the inflation gap is a slightly less clean like-for-like than the LME's.
- **All 13,595 rows kept**, including the 101 with NaN `aod_055` that statsmodels silently dropped from the LME's 13,494. Footnote the 0.75% difference rather than discarding rows the prep stage deliberately kept.

Decided in-flight and flagged:

- **Every metric reported on both scales.** The LME's published R2/within-R2 are *log-scale* quantities, and R2 isn't invariant under a nonlinear transform -- so a raw-scale LightGBM R2 is not the same quantity and isn't comparable to 0.423. The validation script reports raw ug/m3 metrics (native headline) *and* log-scale metrics, to give a genuinely apples-to-apples pair.
- **CV folds mirror the production recipe exactly** (early-stop probe, discard it, refit on the fold's full training set). Keeping the probe would have validated a model trained on ~85% of the stations the production model gets.
- **Tuning blocks keep 2km buffer clusters intact**, closing a residual leak a plain random block split would have left: a held-out station's near-twin sitting in a different block would still have influenced that station's hyperparameters.
- **Drift guard** on `learning_rate`/`max_boosting_rounds`/`early_stopping_rounds`, which are repeated across the three params blocks. A tuned set is only valid for the learning rate it was searched under, so both downstream scripts hard-fail on a mismatch against what the tuning stage recorded.

## Results

> **Superseded later the same day** -- see "Results (continued)" below. The comparison in this section is on the log scale, against the log-fit LME. It was redone in raw ug/m3 against a raw-fit LME, which is the like-for-like comparison of record. The qualitative conclusion (LightGBM wins decisively) is unchanged; the specific figures below are not the ones to cite.

**LightGBM beats the LME on every comparable metric, by a wide margin.** Spatial LOSO, pooled, on the directly-comparable log scale:

| | LME | LightGBM |
|---|---|---|
| R2 | 0.423 | **0.741** |
| within-R2 (Kawano) | 0.211 | **0.576** |
| RMSE ug/m3 | 84.29 (Duan-corrected) | **33.73** |
| MAE ug/m3 | 42.14 (Duan-corrected) | **20.76** |

LightGBM's native raw-scale headline: spatial LOSO R2 = 0.803, within-R2 = 0.721. Out-of-site RMSE is roughly **halved**.

**The spatial-leakage gap is ~2.6x smaller for LightGBM** (+0.081 log-scale, 0.741 -> 0.822) than for the LME (+0.211, 0.423 -> 0.634). The inflation finding replicates, but the LME's random intercept is a pure memorized per-station offset -- unavailable for an unseen station, so it contributes nothing under LOSO and everything under random CV. LightGBM has no such term and must learn cross-station structure either way.

**The two LME outlier stations are resolved by the model change alone:**

| Station | LME R2 | LightGBM R2 |
|---|---|---|
| 5598 (Sector-125, Noida) | **-3.428** | **0.684** |
| 6934 (Karni Singh Shooting Range) | **-1.444** | **0.875** |

This confirms the DAY9 land-use-extrapolation diagnosis from the other direction: both stations sit outside the other 41's covariate range, a linear model extrapolates its fitted coefficients into unseen territory (station 5598's LME RMSE was 422.6 ug/m3, ~4x Delhi's typical level), and a tree ensemble simply cannot extrapolate -- it clamps to the nearest leaf region instead. Here that inability is a virtue. Side effect worth raising: this weakens the case for still reporting the LME's excl-outlier-stations sensitivity run, since LightGBM needs no such companion number.

**New weakness, opposite in kind.** Only 2 of 42 folds are negative -- 5630 Shadipur (-0.111) and 6359 IHBAS (-0.037) -- and both are stations where the LME is positive. (This paragraph originally reported a systematic degradation with buffer-exclusion count, 0.799/0.755/0.542 for 0/1/2 excluded neighbours; **that gradient was later found to be an artifact of these two stations and the claim was withdrawn** -- see the correction in `docs/logs/tasks/10-LightGBM_Model.md`.)

**Feature importance**: season 32.8% of gain, then temperature 16.1%, `aod_055` 10.5%, BLH 10.1%, RH 9.8%, wind 7.7%. Season + meteorology + AOD = **87.0%** of total gain; all 13 static land-use features together = **8.3%**. Independently echoes the LME's finding that no land-use fixed effect reached significance -- two very different model families agreeing the static site covariates carry little weight.

## Work done (continued, same day): raw ug/m3 comparison + raw-target LME

Muhammed's call: the LME-vs-LightGBM comparison should be reported in physical units, not on the log scale, since raw PM2.5 is the quantity of interest. Two pieces of work.

**1. Raw ug/m3 metrics added to the LME validation** (`scripts/modeling/lme/02_validate_lme_cv.py`)

- Added `r2_ugm3` / `within_r2_ugm3` / `rmse_ugm3` / `mae_ugm3` alongside the existing log-scale block, plus `r2_ugm3_naive` and `r2_ugm3_duan` next to the existing naive/Duan RMSE/MAE pair. Needed because R2 is not invariant under a nonlinear transform -- the LME's log-scale 0.423 and LightGBM's raw 0.803 are different quantities and were never validly comparable.
- Only the Duan flavour of `within_r2_ugm3` is computed: Kawano's within-R2 is a squared Pearson correlation, hence invariant to multiplying predictions by a positive constant, which is exactly what Duan's correction does.
- Generalized `compute_group_means(df)` to take a values array so it can build raw-scale station x season means.

**Finding: the log-fit LME's raw-scale spatial-LOSO R2 is negative (-0.226).** Its RMSE of 84.29 ug/m3 already exceeds the data's own sd of 76.12, so `R2 = 1 - RMSE^2/Var` is forced below zero -- in physical units, the log-fit model's out-of-site predictions are worse than predicting the citywide mean. Not a bug; raw-scale R2 is dominated by large absolute errors and station 5598's 422.6 ug/m3 RMSE dominates everything. The excl-outlier-stations variant flips to +0.515, confirming those two stations are entirely responsible.

**2. A raw-target LME variant** (`reports/lme/primary_raw_target/`, 3 new dvc stages, 71 total)

Motivation is fairness, not flattery: scoring a log-fit model on raw compares LightGBM (fit raw, scored raw) against an LME handicapped by a back-transform artifact rather than by any real modelling deficiency. Added `--target_transform {log,raw}` to both LME scripts; on `raw` the validation script skips the back-transform and Duan smearing entirely (mandatory -- `np.exp()` on a raw PM2.5 value overflows). New `prepare_lme_dataset_raw_target` stage using the prep script's already-supported `raw` option.

Also added residual-heteroscedasticity diagnostics (residual variance by fitted-value quintile) to `01_fit_lme_model.py`, written into the existing summary output so no stage schema changed.

## Key decisions (continued)

- **The head-to-head is reported entirely in raw ug/m3, against the raw-fit LME.** Both models then fit AND scored on the same physical scale. LightGBM's log-scale metric set was removed from `02_validate_lightgbm_cv.py` as redundant once the LME reported raw directly.
- **Two LME models, two purposes.** The **raw-fit** LME is the predictive comparator; the **log-fit** LME remains the inference model and the only one whose coefficient table should be interpreted. Justification is now measured, not assumed: residual variance across fitted-value quintiles grows **1.77x** on the log fit but **12.79x** on the raw fit, almost exactly reproducing DAY8 section 9's exploratory ~12.8x estimate. That heteroscedasticity invalidates the raw fit's SEs/p-values/CIs but **not** its point predictions or CV metrics -- which is precisely why the split works.
- **AIC/BIC are not comparable across target transforms** (different response variable, different likelihood scale). Both scripts now print and write this warning; the raw fit's AIC of 143927.3 must never be tabled against the log fit's 20320.9.
- The raw-target fit is **not** registered to the Model Registry -- diagnostic/alternate-specification variant, same treatment as the other four.
- **Reports layout stays flat**: `reports/lme/primary_raw_target/` as a sixth sibling folder. Considered promoting it to a co-primary nested layout, deferred until the number justified it (it does not obviously -- see Results).

## Results (continued)

**Refitting the LME on raw moves its spatial-LOSO R2 from -0.226 to +0.437**, but LightGBM still wins decisively:

| Spatial LOSO, raw ug/m3 | LME log-fit (Duan) | LME raw-fit | LightGBM |
|---|---|---|---|
| R2 | -0.226 | 0.437 | **0.803** |
| within-R2 (Kawano) | 0.167 | 0.220 | **0.721** |
| RMSE | 84.29 | 57.10 | **33.73** |
| MAE | 42.14 | 38.34 | **20.76** |

Out-of-site RMSE 41% lower than the fairly-fit LME (57.10 -> 33.73 ug/m3); within-R2 more than triples. Random CV: LME raw-fit 0.567 vs LightGBM 0.882. Leakage gap on the like-for-like raw comparison is +0.130 (LME) vs +0.079 (LightGBM) -- same qualitative finding as the log-scale version suggested, smaller magnitude than the +0.211 vs +0.081 figures quoted earlier today.

**Refitting on raw does NOT fix the two outlier stations** -- 5598 goes -3.428 -> -2.340 and 6934 -1.444 -> -0.966, both still badly negative. Station 5598's RMSE falls from 422.6 to 138.8 ug/m3 (the `exp()` amplification is gone) but the failure itself survives. This sharpens the DAY9 diagnosis: the problem is linear extrapolation into unseen land-use covariate space, a property of the **model class, not the target scale**, so no transform can address it.

**LightGBM beats the raw-fit LME on 40 of the 42 spatial-LOSO folds** (median fold R2 0.850 vs 0.529). The only two exceptions are 5630 and 6359 -- exactly the buffer-excluded stations where LightGBM went negative. The two models' failures fall on disjoint station sets, which is real supporting evidence for the complementary-failure hypothesis (though not yet proof: "buffer-excluded" and "intrinsically hard" are still confounded).

**Verification**: every pre-existing log-scale CV metric and every AIC/BIC reproduced its documented value to 3+ decimals across all 5 log-fit variants, confirming the changes were purely additive.
